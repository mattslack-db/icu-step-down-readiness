The problem

A blocked ICU bed is a clinical and financial bottleneck

70–90%
Typical ICU occupancy — one blocked bed delays a critical admission
Manual
Step-down decisions are subjective and vary by provider and shift
"To be safe"
Clinicians hold patients longer than needed, lengthening stays

The question every intensivist asks on rounds — “who can safely move to step-down today?” — has no consistent, data-driven answer.

Why it matters beyond the unit

ICU length of stay is a margin, benchmarking, and market-share lever

Payer & margin risk

Under DRG-fixed and value-based contracts, every avoidable ICU day is unreimbursed cost, and ICU bounce-backs feed readmission penalties and quality scores (CMS HRRP, star ratings) that payers price on.

Peer benchmarking

ICU LOS, occupancy, and throughput are ranked against peer hospitals (Vizient, CMS, Leapfrog). Being an LOS outlier is a board- and payer-visible signal of inefficiency.

Competitive position & growth

A full ICU means diverted ambulances, declined tertiary referrals, and postponed elective surgery — lost revenue, lost referral relationships, and lost market share. Freed capacity is growth capacity.

The intensivist's daily step-down call, aggregated across the unit, is what a CFO, CMO, and market strategist see as reimbursement exposure, a benchmark percentile, and the ability to say yes to the next transfer.

The product

A ranked ICU census the care team acts on

  • Census dashboard — every current patient ranked by a relative readiness index (re-scored within minutes as new vitals stream in), color-banded Ready / Borderline / Not-ready.
  • Patient detail — the factors driving each recommendation, vital-sign trends, and a plain-language AI clinical summary.
  • Analytics — capacity and outcome trends, with honest model performance (v7: AUC 0.74, 0.64 readmission-only).

For the CMIO & ICU Medical Director

Built to be trusted, governed, and operated

Governed by design

Unity Catalog across the medallion — DOB dropped, identifiers pseudonymized via UC column masks, PII classification tags, least-privilege grants, full bronze→gold lineage with continuous audit.

Live-fed & operationally served

Continuous Lakeflow streaming pipeline — new vitals in bronze in seconds, scores in ~2 min; pseudonymised gold synced to Lakebase (Postgres) for low-latency app reads; model served on Mosaic AI; narrative via Foundation Model API.

Reproducible

Everything deploys via Databricks Asset Bundles; 199 backend + 38 frontend + 71 pipeline/ML unit + 28 live integration tests, run output committed.

Safe by framing

Public de-identified MIMIC; a relative decision-support index, not an automated discharge gate.

Model honesty

Decision support, not a discharge switch

AUC 0.74
95% CI 0.71–0.76 on patients who left the ICU alive — the only patients a step-down decision is made for. Flags not-safe step-downs at ~5× the base rate
Readmission-only 0.64
72h bounce-back alone is genuinely hard from structured data. We say so, rather than hide it
Index, not %
Relative readiness rank within the current census — never a "probability of safe discharge"

Explainable: SHAP factors in clinical language; known risks always shown as risks, and a patient on vasopressors or a ventilator in the last 24h is never banded Ready. We retired an earlier 0.89: it counted patients who died in the ICU, whose final day is easy to recognise. The next lift comes from your data, notes and validation with your clinicians.

Project plan

Trained on your history, proven on your unit — before anyone relies on it

Weeks 1–4 · Train on your history Weeks 5–8 · Silent pilot Weeks 9–16 · Clinician pilot Week 17+ · Scale
Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4 Train on your historical data
  • Point the same governed pipeline at your EHR feed — 2–3 years of your ICU stays
  • Retrain on your patients, practice and outcomes
  • ICU Medical Director co-defines features, guardrails and the “safe step-down” label
Exit gate: discrimination and calibration on your held-out stays, checked by unit and patient subgroup
Phase 2 · Weeks 5–8 Silent pilot
  • Live scores every shift, not shown to clinicians
  • Compared with the step-down calls clinicians actually made, and 72h outcomes
  • Validates the numbers on today's patients, not just history
Exit gate: live performance matches retrospective; no safety signal in Ready-banded patients
Phase 3 · Weeks 9–16 Clinician pilot — one ICU
  • Ranked census used on rounds; the decision stays with the clinician
  • Clinicians record agree / disagree and why
  • Weekly review of flagged vs actual bounce-backs builds trust and tunes thresholds
Exit gate: ICU LOS reduced with safe-transfer and 72h bounce-back rates held or improved
Phase 4 · Week 17+ Decide & scale
  • Go / no-go with clinical governance
  • Extend unit by unit on the same platform
  • Drift monitoring and scheduled retraining
Ongoing: performance reported to the clinical governance board

Clinicians see nothing until the model has been validated twice: first on your history, then silently on live patients. Trust is earned before the index is used on rounds.
